Tankless Water Heater Repair Cost in Colton, CA

.
By the Colton Express Plumbing team · Updated October 24, 2026
.

Tankless water heater repair cost in Colton depends on the fault. A HomeAdvisor guide titled for 2025 reports an average of $600 nationally, with a typical range of $75 to $1,300 and complex repairs up to $1,950. Flushing and descaling is listed at $150 to $300.

In Colton, the first thing to check is scale. The City's water is very hard, and a tankless unit has narrow heat exchanger passages that scale can restrict. This guide covers error codes, common faults, a flush you can ask for and how to decide between repair and replacement.

Symptoms that point to a tankless repair

.

Tankless units fail differently from tanks. These are common patterns.

Lukewarm or fluctuating water

Hot water cools in the middle of a shower, then returns. This can mean a scaled heat exchanger, a weak flow sensor reading or an undersized gas supply.

Error code on the display

The unit shuts down and flashes a number or letter code. The code names the circuit or sensor that tripped, not always the part that failed.

Cold water sandwich

A burst of hot, then cold, then hot water usually happens when the unit shuts off and restarts. Scale, low flow or a dirty inlet screen may be behind it.

Unit will not fire

Water runs, the fan spins, but there is no flame. Igniter, flame rod, gas supply or venting problems are common suspects.

Noise or rumbling

Popping or kettle sounds point to scale boiling inside the heat exchanger.

Common tankless faults, causes and who fixes them

.

Error code meanings differ by brand, so use this as a map and check your manual for exact codes.

Fault area.Typical cause.Owner or plumber.
Ignition failure code.Dirty or worn igniter or flame rod, gas supply issue.Plumber for gas-side parts.
Flow or minimum flow code.Clogged inlet screen, flow sensor dirt, low pressure.Owner can clean the screen, plumber for the sensor.
Overheat or high temperature code.Scaled heat exchanger, failed thermistor.Plumber, often after a flush.
Vent or exhaust code.Blocked vent, fan fault, wrong vent length.Plumber.
Water leak or condensate code.Failed seal, cracked fitting, blocked condensate drain.Plumber.

Never reset a unit repeatedly after an ignition or vent code. Repeated attempts can send unburned gas into the vent.

Where the money goes in a tankless repair

.

The HomeAdvisor guide lists several repair price bands. A fuse is $50 to $75, a pilot light $75 to $150, a thermostat $75 to $150 and a vent $50 to $125. A gas valve replacement is listed near $350, and complex leak repairs can approach $1,000.

The page does not give prices for igniters or flow sensors, so we do not either. Any repair estimate also depends on the model, parts supply and labor time. These are national numbers, not Colton Express Plumbing prices.

Because tankless units depend on electronics, a diagnostic visit usually comes first. A written price follows once the tech knows which part failed.

Why Colton water is hard on a tankless heater

.

The City of Colton's 2024 water quality report shows total hardness averaging about 207 mg/L, in a range from 150 to 230. That is roughly 12 grains per gallon, and the USGS scale calls anything over 180 mg/L very hard.

A tankless heat exchanger heats water fast through thin passages. Hard water leaves scale in those passages, which restricts flow and insulates the metal. That can trigger overheat codes, reduce output and shorten the unit's life.

A regular flush is the usual defense, as the maker's manual describes. We do not set a Colton schedule for it, so follow the manual and ask the tech to inspect the heat exchanger at each visit.

How a descaling flush works on a tankless unit

.

Many manufacturers describe this process, and a plumber can do it if you do not want to.

  1. Shut off power and gas. Turn off the unit and close the gas valve and both water isolation valves.
  2. Connect a pump and hoses. Attach hoses to the service valves, with one line to a bucket of descaling solution and one back into it.
  3. Circulate the solution. Run the pump for the time the manual says, often about an hour, so the acid dissolves the scale.
  4. Rinse with clean water. Flush the unit so no solution stays in the heat exchanger or lines.
  5. Clean the inlet screen. Pull the filter screen and rinse off debris, then reassemble and reopen the valves slowly.
  6. Test the unit. Open a hot tap, check for leaks and watch the display for codes.

Brand and model Some brands use proprietary parts that cost more or take longer to source.
Failed component A sensor or fuse differs from a heat exchanger or gas valve. The HomeAdvisor guide lists a wide spread.
Scale level A heavily scaled unit may need a flush before the real fault shows.
Gas and venting condition Venting, gas line sizing and condensate drains add checks and sometimes parts.
Age of the unit The older the unit, the more likely other parts are near the end of life.
Homeowners policies often cover sudden and accidental damage, such as a leak that ruins a wall. They often exclude wear, corrosion or slow leaks. Read your policy and photograph any damage before cleanup.

Repair or replace: a decision guide

.

HomeAdvisor says repair is generally the better choice for a unit under 10 years old with no major past breakdowns. It suggests considering replacement if repair costs exceed half the price of a new system, especially past 15 years. Frequent error codes and persistent leaks also point toward replacement.

A cracked heat exchanger is the usual end of life event, because that part is a major share of the unit's cost. If your unit shows scale damage and a cracked exchanger, replacement is cleaner. If the fault is a sensor or igniter, a repair is usually sensible.

Local note.

Colton gas line and permit points for tankless

.

The City of Colton's Building Division water heater permit page notes that tankless units can need up to five times the BTU input of a standard heater. That means the gas line may need upsizing. If your unit keeps tripping on ignition or low gas, ask the plumber to check the gas line size against the manufacturer's specification.

The same page says outdoor installs need Planning approval first, and a new location needs rough plumbing and final inspections. Ask the City of Colton Building Division, at 659 North La Cadena Drive, whether your replacement or repair needs a permit.

Test your tap and ask your water utility for its annual report, since hardness can vary by address within the system. We do not diagnose health effects of water.
