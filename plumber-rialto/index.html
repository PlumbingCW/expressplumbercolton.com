Five plumbing problems that send Rialto homeowners to the phone

.

This list is our reading of housing age and water type in the city, not a count of past jobs.

Hot water that fades or stops

Rialto groundwater is hard, and scale builds on tank bottoms and heating parts. A heater that rumbles or runs out fast is often coated in it.

Pinhole leaks and damp drywall

Homes from the late 1970s through the mid 1990s may still carry polybutylene supply pipe. Leaks tend to show first at the joints, so a wet spot near a fitting deserves a look.

Slow drains that keep coming back

Kitchen lines slow when grease coats the pipe wall and shrinks the opening. If every drain in the house slows at once, the trouble sits further down the line.

A water meter that moves with every tap off

Close every fixture and watch the meter's leak indicator or low-flow dial. Any movement means water is escaping somewhere between the meter and the house or inside it.

A backup in the lowest drain

The lowest drain in a house, such as a shower or floor drain, shows a blocked main line first. Wastewater rising there is a reason to stop using water and call.

Burst pipe or sewage backup in Rialto: what to do while you wait

.

If water is spraying, pooling or rising out of a drain, call the 24/7 line right away. Dispatch is open at all hours, and the dispatcher will ask what you can see and where the water is going.

These steps limit the damage until a plumber is on site.

  • Shut off the main valve. Look near the meter or where the line enters the house, and turn the handle clockwise. If it will not move, stop and tell the dispatcher rather than forcing it.
  • Stop using every drain. Do not run the sink, shower or washer during a backup. Each gallon you add has nowhere to go but the floor.
  • Keep clear of wet electrical areas. If water reaches outlets, a panel or appliances, leave the area. We do not do electrical work, so an electrician must check anything that got wet.
  • Treat a gas smell separately. If you smell gas near a water heater, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first. Then call us.
  • Photograph before you clean. Take pictures of the damage and the failed part for your insurance claim, then move belongings out of the wet zone.

Why a 1980s Rialto house deserves a closer look at its pipes

.

About 32 percent of Rialto homes went up between 1980 and 1989, the biggest single decade in the city. That figure comes from the Census Bureau's American Community Survey for 2020 to 2024. The 1970s and 1990s add another large share.

One pipe material matters across that stretch. An insurer's loss-control bulletin says polybutylene supply pipe was used widely from 1979 until about 1995. It says chlorine and other oxidants are believed to make the pipe flake and turn brittle, with most leaks at the joints. West Valley Water District reports chlorine as its disinfectant.

You can check by eye. Look near the water heater and under sinks for gray, blue or black plastic pipe with a gray or white plastic fitting held by a metal band. Copper stubs at the fixtures do not rule it out, because copper is often joined to the plastic further back. Many Rialto homes have been repiped by now, and nothing seen from the street tells us which ones.

If the plastic is there, a plumber can confirm it and plan a repipe on your schedule instead of a leak's. A hidden wet spot is a job for leak detection first.

Whose pipes feed your Rialto tap: the city, West Valley, or both

.

Two public systems sell water inside Rialto. West Valley Water District's planning document describes its service area as northern and southern sections, with the central portion in between served by the City of Rialto. The sources we checked do not map individual streets, so the water bill on your counter is the real answer.

That bill also tells you who to call about a main break in the street or a pressure drop that hits the whole block. Ask the utility where its responsibility ends and yours begins. A leak inside the property is where a plumber works.

Rialto's perchlorate history is real. The chemical leaked from a 160-acre site in the late 1990s, and the EPA designed a groundwater pump and treat system.

In practice the treatment sits at the utility's wells. West Valley's 2024 report lists perchlorate in its distribution system between not detected and 0.59 micrograms per liter, against a state limit of 6.0, and says wells 41 and 42 use ion exchange. Nothing inside your house needs fixing for it, and we do not diagnose health effects of water.

Hardness is what your plumbing feels. The same report shows hardness from 130 to 250 milligrams per liter across its sources, with groundwater readings of 240 to 250. That runs from hard to very hard on the USGS scale. If the City of Rialto serves you, ask for its annual water quality report, because we could not confirm its figures.

The City of Rialto runs the sewer system and its own treatment plant, and the water district's plan says that plant handles most wastewater collected in its area. Before you pay for a sewer repair, ask the city where the public main ends and your private line begins.

What to have ready before a Rialto plumber arrives

.

A few minutes of preparation shortens the visit.

  • Your water bill. It shows whether West Valley Water District or the City of Rialto serves you, which matters if the problem starts at the meter.
  • The year the house was built. It points to the likely pipe material, from galvanized in older homes to polybutylene in the 1979 to 1995 window.
  • The main shutoff location. Walk to it once and confirm it turns. A stuck valve is better found before an emergency.
  • Notes and photos. Write down when the problem started, which fixtures are affected and whether the meter moves with everything off.
  • A clear path. Clear access to the water heater, the cleanout and the cabinets under sinks. Same-day appointments are offered when available, and a clear path saves time on site.
