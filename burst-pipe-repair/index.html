Cracked, split or pinholed: what each failure looks like

.

Different breaks need different responses, so it helps to describe what you see.

Spraying or gushing water

A split or blown fitting. Shut the main valve immediately, because every minute can add water to the structure.

Steady drip from a pipe or joint

A pinhole, a loose compression fitting or a worn solder joint. It looks small but can wet insulation and framing for weeks.

Water stain growing on a ceiling or wall

A pipe is leaking out of sight. Shut the water, then have the line located before the wall is opened.

No water at all, or a broken shutoff

A broken water valve, a failed supply line or a closed main can leave taps dry. A valve that snapped or will not turn needs repair or replacement.

Wet soil or a bubbling lawn

A break in the buried line. See water line repair for service line work.

Why pipes burst in a mild-winter city

.

Freezing is the cause most people expect, but frozen pipes are less likely in a mild-winter climate like Colton's. As general plumbing reasoning, a burst here more often traces to corrosion that thinned the wall, a joint that failed, or water pressure that was too high for the pipe. The pressure regulator, if there is one, is part of the picture.

Corrosion works slowly. A copper pipe can develop pinholes from the inside, and galvanized steel narrows and rusts from within. Joints fail where solder or a fitting was imperfect or where movement wore it over years. A sudden surge, such as a water hammer from a fast-closing valve, can then finish the job.

That matters for repair, because patching one burst without checking the rest invites the next failure. A technician looks at the cut ends, the nearby fittings and the pressure before closing up the wall.

Burst pipe right now: what to do first while the crew is on the way

.

A burst pipe is an emergency. Call the 24/7 line now, since dispatch is open at night and on weekends and same-day appointments are available when schedules allow. Then do the following in order.

  • Close the main water valve. Turn it fully clockwise. It is often near the water heater, in the garage or on the outside wall facing the street.
  • Mind the electricity. Water and power do not mix. If water is near outlets, a ceiling light or the panel, stay away and turn off power only if the breaker is dry and reachable.
  • Drain the lines. With the main closed, open a faucet at the lowest point and one at the highest. This releases pressure and drains the pipes so less water escapes.
  • Turn off the water heater. If the water supply is cut, an electric heater can overheat when the tank empties. Switch off the breaker or set a gas heater to pilot.
  • Stop the damage and record it. Move belongings, scoop standing water if safe and photograph or film all wet areas before cleanup.

Copper, PEX and drain pipe: how each kind is repaired

.

Copper pipe repair often means cutting out the damaged section and soldering or pressing in a new piece with proper fittings. If corrosion has thinned the pipe over a long stretch, replacing the full run is more durable than patching a single hole. Mixed metals, such as copper joined to steel without a dielectric fitting, can corrode at the connection, which a technician checks.

PEX and plastic supply lines are repaired with crimp, clamp or push fittings sized to the pipe. These materials resist some corrosion, but a failed fitting, a kinked bend or sun exposure can still cause a leak. The fix is usually fast once the leak is exposed.

Drain pipe repair differs because drain pipes are not under pressure. A cracked drain leaks only when water runs through it, so the signs are damp spots after showers or a soggy floor near a fixture. A cracked drain can be repaired by replacing the broken section. For problems farther out in the yard, see sewer line repair.

Moisture and damage records to gather after a burst

.

Good records help your insurance claim and make drying faster.

  • Photos and video. Capture the break, the wet walls, the flooring and the water on the meter before anything is moved.
  • Meter reading. Write down the reading at shutoff so any water loss can be tied to a date and time.
  • Dry quickly. Remove standing water, run fans and a dehumidifier, and lift wet carpet. Damp materials can grow mold in a couple of days.
  • Save what you remove. Keep a section of the failed pipe. It shows the cause and may help the claim.
  • Call your insurer early. Report the loss, ask what they cover and ask whether they want to inspect before cleanup.

What shapes the price of a burst pipe repair

.

A burst pipe repair can be small or large depending on what is damaged and how hard it is to reach. We explain the job and the cost before work begins.

Location of the break. A pipe in an open crawlspace is faster to reach than one inside a wall, ceiling or under flooring.
Pipe material and length. Copper, PEX and galvanized steel use different parts and labor, and a longer section costs more.
Number of weak spots. A single clean failure is a small fix, while pinholes along the line may need a larger replacement.
Access work and restoration. Opening and patching drywall is part of the job, but paint and flooring may be separate.
Time of the call. A middle-of-the-night emergency has different staffing than a scheduled visit.
Homeowners policies often cover sudden and accidental water damage from a burst pipe, including the damage left behind. They often exclude damage from wear, corrosion or slow leaks, and may treat the failed pipe itself differently. Read your policy and document everything before cleanup.
