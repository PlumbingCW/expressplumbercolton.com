When the main drain behind the whole house stops, every fixture feels it at once. Our 24/7 line handles sewer line cleaning in Colton, CA at any hour. While you wait, stop all water use and leave the cleanout cap in place.

Signs the main line is blocked, not one drain

.

A main blockage shows up in more than one place.

Lowest drain backs up first

Water in a shower or floor drain at the lowest level when a toilet upstairs is flushed. Gravity sends the overflow to the lowest opening.

Toilets gurgle when the washer runs

Air is being pushed back through a partly closed main. The sound is a warning that capacity is limited.

Several fixtures drain slowly

A sink, a tub and a toilet all slow together. A single clog would affect one fixture.

Floor drain bubbles or smells

A bubbling basement or garage drain suggests the line beyond it is full.

Water stands in the cleanout

If the cleanout cap is opened by a technician and water is visible, the blockage is downstream of that point.

Where the cleanout is and how a technician uses it

.

The cleanout is a capped pipe, often white or black plastic or older cast iron, that gives direct access to the main. Common places are near the foundation on the exterior wall closest to the street, in a side yard, beside the garage or in a small ground box. Some homes have one inside, in a basement, crawlspace or utility area.

Technicians open the cap slowly and feed a cable or jetting hose along the pipe. Access matters because a cleanout lets the machine start close to the main, instead of entering through a toilet. Without a cleanout, the toilet or a roof vent may be the entry point, which takes more time and care.

If you cannot find it, describe where the sewer line leaves the house. A technician can locate it, and if a cleanout is missing, adding one is a practical improvement.

Main line backed up right now: what to do and what not to do

.

A backed-up main line is an emergency because sewage can rise in the lowest fixtures. Call the 24/7 line. We schedule same-day and after-hours appointments when available and ask a few questions so the right cable or jetter goes on the truck.

  • Stop water use house-wide. That includes laundry, dishwashers, showers and toilet flushing. The line is full.
  • Do not open the cleanout cap. A full line can spill out of it. A technician will open it with protection in place.
  • Do not pour chemicals. They sit in the pipe and make later work more hazardous.
  • Do not rent a drum machine on your own. A rented cable can wedge in a joint, tear a liner or injure the user, and it cannot show what is happening inside.
  • Protect floors and belongings. Move items from lower rooms, and keep people and pets out of areas where water has risen.

Cable or jetting through the cleanout: how a main is cleared

.

For most main-line stoppages the first tool is a large-diameter cable on a drum machine. The head is chosen for the blockage. A spiral head retrieves a plug, a blade cutter slices through roots and a spear head penetrates a dense mat. Cable work opens the line quickly.

When the problem is a coating, a mix of grease and paper, or fine root hairs, jetting follows. The water scours the wall and flushes debris to the public main. Our hydro jetting page explains the method and the pipe condition it needs.

Both are followed by a camera look when the cause is not obvious. The video tells us whether the line is clean and sound or whether something else lies behind the clog.

Mistakes that turn a blocked main into a worse problem

.

These habits clog mains or make a blockage harder to clear.

  • Flushing wipes labeled flushable. They do not break down like paper and tangle with roots and grease into dense plugs.
  • Pouring grease down the kitchen drain. It travels warm and sticks as it cools. Wipe pans and bin the fat.
  • Ignoring early gurgling. A partial blockage is easier and cheaper to clear than a full one.
  • Blocking the cleanout. Piling soil, planters or a patio over it can delay emergency work.
  • Skipping maintenance on a line with roots. A home with known root intrusion needs regular clearing or a repair to end the cycle.

What shapes the price of clearing a main line

.

A main-line cleaning is priced by access, machine and blockage, not by a single flat figure.

Cleanout access. A clear exterior cleanout is the simplest entry. Removing a toilet or opening a vent adds work.
Length of line. A longer run to the street needs more cable, more time and sometimes more than one pass.
Nature of the blockage. Paper clears quickly. Roots, grease and heavy scale call for cutters or jetting.
Equipment used. A cable machine and a jetter are different rigs, and the choice depends on the cause.
Time and follow-up. Night and weekend calls are staffed differently, and a camera look afterward adds time.
Homeowners policies often cover sudden and accidental damage and often exclude wear, corrosion or slow leaks. Cleaning a blocked line is usually maintenance, but water damage from a sudden backup may be covered. Read your policy and photograph damage before cleanup.
A clear written price comes before work starts, and you decide whether to go ahead.
