Water Heater Installation Cost in Colton, CA: Average Price and What Moves It

.
By the Colton Express Plumbing team · Updated October 24, 2026
.

A 2026 This Old House cost guide puts the average water heater installation at about $1,950, with a typical range of $1,600 to $5,400. In Colton, CA, where your job lands in that range depends on tank or tankless, venting, gas line size, the permit, and removal of the old unit.

Those figures are national and are not a Colton Express Plumbing quote. This guide shows what builds the total, which local permit steps apply, and what to ask before you sign. For the job itself, see our water heater installation page.

Tank, tankless, or hybrid: where the first big gap opens

.

A standard tank is the lowest-cost route because the plumbing and the vent usually already exist. A tankless unit costs more up front, takes more labor, and often needs larger venting and a bigger gas supply. A hybrid, or heat pump, unit uses electricity to move heat and needs space, a drain for condensate, and a suitable circuit.

Size matters inside each type. A household that runs a shower and a dishwasher at once needs a larger tank, or a tankless unit rated for the flow of those fixtures together. Sizing from the number of bathrooms and peak use, not from what fit in the old closet, avoids both cold showers and wasted fuel.

Six line items that can change the quote

.

Ask the installer to list each one so you can compare bids on equal terms.

  • Venting. A new vent route, a liner, or a power vent adds parts and labor. Moving the heater to a new spot nearly always raises this line.
  • Gas line size. Tankless units draw far more gas at once, so the old pipe may be too small. Upsizing means more pipe, more hours, and an inspection.
  • Code upgrades. Ask whether seismic strapping, a drain pan, an expansion tank, and a proper discharge line are part of the job and which code edition applies. Colton's Building Division can say what it enforces.
  • Shutoff and connectors. A seized old valve or corroded connectors get replaced during the swap, so a small parts charge is common.
  • Access. A heater in a tight attic or closet takes longer to remove and set. Stairs add time too.
  • Permit and haul-away. Both appear as separate lines or fold into the total. Confirm which.

Why a quote can double without anything being wrong

.

Two bids can look far apart because they are not the same job. One may reuse the existing vent and drain pan, and the other may replace them. One may include permit handling and the other may leave it to you.

Be wary of a price that is far below the rest and has no line items. Missing items tend to show up as add-ons after the old heater is already drained. A written quote with the unit model, vent plan, permit, and haul-away makes that hard to do.

Local note.

Colton permit steps that shape the price and the schedule

.

The City of Colton's water heater permit page says the owner or an insured California-licensed contractor can pull the permit. Plumbing work falls under the C-36 license class, and you can check any license number on the Contractors State License Board site before work starts. A hire who cannot give a number is a red flag.

The city requires an application, ID, a signed CF1R-ALT-01-E energy form, and a site plan. A change-out in the same location gets one final inspection, while a new location gets a rough plumbing inspection and a final. The practical takeaway: a move to a new spot adds an inspection visit, so expect more time and cost.

The same page says gas-to-electric conversion is not allowed under the alteration path, and outdoor installs need Planning approval first. It also notes that tankless units can need up to five times the BTU input of a standard heater, so the gas line may need upsizing. It does not list fees, so ask the Building Division at the City for the current charge. To schedule an inspection for the next day, the city says to call by 4 pm.

Colton's water averages about 207 mg/L hardness in the city's Consumer Confidence Report, which is very hard on the USGS scale. Scale insulates the heating surface and cuts efficiency, so a sediment-prone tank may work harder. Ask the installer about a flush plan or a softener in the same visit.

Before you approve a water heater quote

.

These checks take a few minutes and prevent most disputes.

  1. Check the license. Look up the contractor's number on the CSLB website and confirm the C-36 plumbing classification.
  2. Ask who pulls the permit. The contractor should name the permit and inspection plan, not leave it to you.
  3. Request line items. Unit model, vent, gas work, code items, permit, and haul-away should each appear.
  4. Confirm the fuel plan. Staying on gas keeps the job on the simple path. Switching fuels changes the permit route.
  5. Get the warranty terms in writing. Ask what the manufacturer covers and what labor coverage the installer offers.
