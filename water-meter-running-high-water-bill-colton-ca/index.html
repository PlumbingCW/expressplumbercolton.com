Water Meter Running With Everything Off: Finding the Leak Behind a High Water Bill in Colton, CA

.
By the Colton Express Plumbing team · Updated October 24, 2026
.

If your water meter keeps moving after every tap is off, water is going somewhere inside or outside your Colton, CA home. Shut off all fixtures, read the meter, wait fifteen minutes with no use, and read it again. Any change, or a spinning low-flow indicator, means a leak.

The rest of this guide shows the full test, how to split the leak between the house and the yard, and the usual hidden causes. It also covers what the City of Colton water utility can tell you. When the leak is hard to trace, our leak detection service uses listening and thermal tools.

The meter test, step by step

.

You need about twenty minutes and a pen. Do it when nobody is home or asleep.

  1. Find the meter. It usually sits in a ground box near the curb or the front of the lot. Lift the lid with a screwdriver and watch for insects.
  2. Shut off all water use. Stop the dishwasher, washer, ice maker, softener cycle, and irrigation. Make sure no one flushes or runs a tap.
  3. Look at the low-flow indicator. Many meters have a small triangle or star dial that spins at the slightest flow. If it turns with everything off, water is moving.
  4. Record the reading. Write down the digits, or take a photo, then wait fifteen to thirty minutes with no water use and read again.
  5. Split house from yard. Close the main valve at the house and watch the meter. If it stops, the leak is inside. If it keeps moving, the leak is on the line between the meter and the house, or at the meter.

Hidden leaks that quietly drive up a bill

.

Most high bills come from a few repeat offenders, and they are often silent.

  • Toilet flapper. Water slips past a worn flapper into the bowl without any sound. Add dye to the tank, wait fifteen minutes, and see if color reaches the bowl. If it needs replacing, see our notes on toilet repair.
  • Irrigation. A cracked line, a stuck valve, or a head spraying a wall can run for hours. Look for wet ground or green patches along the line.
  • Water heater relief valve. A pressure relief valve that weeps sends water to a drain or outside.
  • Slab or yard line. A pinhole in a buried line may show only as a warm spot, a wet stripe, or a hissing sound at the meter.
  • Softener or filter. A stuck regeneration valve can drain slowly all day.
  • Dripping fixtures. A faucet that drips once per second can add up over a month.

Reading your usage on the bill and the meter

.

Compare the current bill to the same cycle last year, and look for a step change, not a gradual drift. A leak often shows as a sudden rise that stays high, while seasonal irrigation rises and falls. A spike for one cycle that then disappears can mean a one-time fill, such as a pool.

Check the units on your bill and on your meter. Some meters read in cubic feet and some in gallons, and mixing them up leads to wrong conclusions. If you are unsure, ask the utility what unit your meter and bill use.

When to call a plumber and when to call the utility

.

If the leak is before the meter, it is the utility's side. If it is after the meter, it is generally yours. Ask the City where the dividing point is at your address.

For a leak inside the house, a plumber can isolate each branch with pressure checks and listening gear. Moisture or a warm floor spot can indicate a slab leak, where the pipe runs under concrete. We find the source first and explain the options before any cutting starts.

What to ask the City of Colton water utility

.

The City of Colton Water Utilities Department runs the meter and the bill, and its customer service line is on your bill and on the city website. Ask three questions: can they check your meter for a stuck or faulty reading, can they pull your usage history, and what is the process to request a leak check.

We do not state any billing adjustment policy here, because we have not verified one. Ask the City whether any adjustment or review exists, what proof it wants, and how long a request takes. Keep the plumber's invoice and your meter photos, since they are the proof most utilities ask for.

Colton water facts that affect leak hunting

.

The City of Colton's Consumer Confidence Report says the Water Utilities Department serves about 90 percent of the city through more than 10,500 connections. So check your bill to confirm that the City, and not another provider, is your water supplier before you call it. A different provider has a different meter and a different phone line.

The report lists hardness averaging about 207 mg/L, which is very hard on the USGS scale. Scale builds on toilet fill valves and flappers and keeps them from seating, so a toilet leak is a good first suspect. Wipe a flapper with your finger: a gritty film is a sign it may need replacing.

The City's housing data shows about 52 percent of homes were built before 1980 and about 49.6 percent of homes are rented. Older homes mean older supply lines and shutoffs, and renters should tell their landlord about a meter that moves, since the lease usually says who calls the plumber.

If you live just outside the city line, such as in Grand Terrace or Bloomington, a different water provider handles your meter, so start with the name on your bill. Pressure regulators and shutoff valves installed after a leak is found are covered by the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).
