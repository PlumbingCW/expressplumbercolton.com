Sewer Line Crack Repair in Colton, CA: Finding It, Fixing It, Choosing a Method

.
By the Colton Express Plumbing team · Updated October 24, 2026
.

A cracked sewer line in Colton, CA is found with a camera run through the pipe, then fixed by one of three methods: a spot repair, a liner, or a full replacement. Which one fits depends on how many cracks there are, where they sit, and what the pipe is made of.

This guide explains why sewer lines crack, how the camera locates the damage, and the trade-offs between methods. If you already have a backup, jump to the middle section. Our sewer line repair page covers the service itself.

Five reasons a buried sewer pipe cracks

.

Most failures come from a slow process, not a single event.

Tree and shrub roots

Roots follow moisture to a joint, slip inside, and grow. Over time they pry the joint apart and can split the wall.

Ground movement

Soil that settles or shifts bends a rigid pipe. Clay and cast iron crack rather than flex.

Age and wear

Pipe and joints weaken after decades of flow and soil load. Rough interior walls then catch debris.

Cast iron corrosion

Iron rusts from the inside, flaking and thinning the wall until it cracks or collapses.

Offset joints

Where one section sits lower than the next, waste drops into the gap and the edge catches solids, so backups repeat.

How a camera inspection finds the crack

.

A small waterproof camera, the heart of a sewer inspection, on a flexible cable goes in through a cleanout. The tech watches a live feed and notes the footage counter as the camera moves. That number tells them how far from the cleanout the damage is.

A locator at the surface picks up the camera's signal, so the tech can mark the ground above the problem. This matters for a spot repair, because the dig has to be in the right place. Ask for a copy of the video, which also helps with quotes from other contractors.

Cracks usually show as lines, missing pieces, or gaps with soil visible. Roots appear as thin white threads or thick mats. A belly, where the pipe sags and holds water, shows as a pool at the low point.

Spot repair, lining, or replacement: the trade-offs

.

No method wins every time, so match it to the pipe condition.

Method.Best when.Limits.
Spot repair (dig and patch).One or two isolated cracks and a sound pipe elsewhere.Needs excavation at each spot, and other weak spots may remain.
Cured-in-place lining.Cracks or root entry along a pipe that still holds its shape.Cannot fix a collapsed or deeply offset pipe, and it slightly reduces the bore.
Pipe bursting.Old pipe that is failing along its length.Needs entry pits, and nearby utilities must be located.
Full replacement by trench.Collapsed, bellied, or heavily corroded runs.Most disruptive to landscaping and paving.

If sewage is backing up right now

.

Act on these while you wait for a plumber, because a backup spreads quickly.

  1. Stop using water. Do not run the dishwasher, washer, showers, or faucets. Every drop goes toward the blockage.
  2. Keep people and pets away. Sewage contains bacteria. Keep children out of the area and wear gloves and boots if you must enter.
  3. Do not pour chemicals. Drain cleaners will not clear a crack and can burn skin or damage a liner later.
  4. Open the cleanout cap carefully. If backed-up waste is visible there, a pressure release may help, but stay clear because it can spill.
  5. Photograph the damage. Take pictures before cleanup for an insurance claim, and ask the insurer what they cover.

Choosing between a patch and a long-term fix

.

A patch is cheaper today, but it can cost more over time if the rest of the pipe is also tired. A camera video helps here. If the footage shows the same cracks, roots, and flaking every few feet, a single patch buys little.

Lining has a sound track record on pipes with minor cracks and root entry. But it needs a clean, pre-cleared pipe, and it will not solve a sagging section. Replacement costs the most in disruption and gives the cleanest start. In many cases the camera view makes the choice plain.

What shapes a sewer crack repair quote

.

We give no dollar figure here because the range between a spot patch and a long replacement is wide. These factors set the price.

Pipe depth and length Deeper and longer runs mean more digging or more liner.
Surface restoration Concrete, driveway, or landscaping to be cut and rebuilt adds to the job.
Access A working cleanout or a clear path to the pipe speeds the job.
Method Lining, bursting, and trenching each carry a different cost profile.
Permits and inspections Digging in the right-of-way or tying into the main may require them.
Homeowners policies often cover sudden and accidental damage and often exclude wear, corrosion, root intrusion, or slow leaks. Read your policy, and document damage before cleanup. Some insurers sell a separate line coverage rider.
A clear written price comes before work starts, including who handles the permit and any restoration.
