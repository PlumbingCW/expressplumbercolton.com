A city built in layers: San Bernardino homes by decade

.

The largest single block of San Bernardino housing went up in the 1980s, at about 19 percent of all homes. The 1950s follow at about 17 percent and the 1970s at about 14 percent. Around 9 percent date from 1939 or earlier, and another 8 percent from the 1940s.

A third of the city is older than 1960. In neighboring Rialto, that figure is under a fifth.

What galvanized pipe does inside a 1950s San Bernardino house

.

Galvanized steel is iron pipe coated in zinc. East Bay Municipal Utility District says it was commonly used before the 1960s, and that once the zinc wears away the iron rusts and deposits can narrow the pipe. Alameda County Water District puts the typical wear-out window at roughly 40 to 50 years.

From the tap, that looks like weak flow at one fixture, rust-colored water after the house sat idle, or a stain that keeps returning on a wall. Leak detection finds the wet joint without opening walls everywhere. If many joints are failing, a repipe ends the patching cycle, and the written price comes before work starts.

Not every old house has it, since some were repiped in copper long ago. We confirm the material before suggesting anything.

Municipal Water Department or East Valley: which agency bills you

.

The City of San Bernardino Municipal Water Department is the city's own water and sewer utility. East Valley Water District serves the City of Highland and portions of both the city and county of San Bernardino, though its page does not name neighborhoods. Your bill names the right agency, so read it before calling anyone about a street-side problem.

The department's 2025 water quality report says all of its supply comes from the Bunker Hill Groundwater Basin through more than 40 wells. Measured hardness ranged from 130 to 330 milligrams per liter, with an average of 242. That works out to roughly 14 grains per gallon, well into the very hard range, so scale is a steady enemy of water heaters here.

The same report describes a federal Superfund cleanup of old TCE and PCE contamination, where the department treats pumped water with granular activated carbon. It lists average perchlorate, TCE and PCE results under their limits. That work happens at the utility and changes nothing about your pipes, and we do not diagnose health effects of water.

For sewers, the department says it provides sewer collection for the city and neighboring communities. Ask it where the public main ends before you pay for a lateral repair.

Burst line, backed-up sewer or no water in San Bernardino: do this first

.

Water where it should not be is an emergency at any hour. Call the 24/7 line and give the dispatcher your cross streets and what you can see.

Until a plumber arrives, these steps keep the damage small.

  • Close the main valve. In an older house the valve may be stiff from years of sitting. Turn it clockwise gently and do not force it.
  • Leave the drains alone. Skip flushing, laundry and showers during a backup. In a house with a shared line, upstairs water can surface in the lowest fixture.
  • Stay away from wet electrical areas. Do not step into standing water near outlets or a panel. We do not do electrical work, so have an electrician inspect anything that got wet.
  • Gas smell means leave. If you smell gas by a heater or appliance, get everyone outside and call the gas utility or 911 before calling us.
  • Record the damage. Photograph the water, the failed pipe and any soaked belongings before cleanup, since insurers ask for them.

Storm history, the Santa Ana River and the limits of a plumber

.

The Santa Ana River forms part of San Bernardino's southern border, and the county's flood history records that the March 1938 storm reached practically all of the San Bernardino Valley. Floodwater that comes in from outside is an insurance and restoration matter. A plumber starts work after the water is gone and the shutoff is safe.

I-10, I-215 and SR-210 meet inside the city, so crews can come from several directions. We also take calls from the Muscoy area and from Loma Linda across the river to the south.

In the San Bernardino ZIP codes 92405 and 92403, ask about sewer line repair and drain cleaning first when several drains slow at once. In Highland's 92346, just east of the city, start with water heater repair when hot water runs short.

Questions that sort out a San Bernardino plumbing problem before we arrive

.

Answering these on the phone helps the dispatcher match the right tools to the job.

  • When was the house built? The decade points to the likely supply pipe, from galvanized to polybutylene to newer plastic and copper.
  • Which agency sends your water bill? The Municipal Water Department and East Valley Water District serve different parts of the area.
  • Is it one fixture or the whole house? One slow drain points to a branch line. Every drain slow at once points to the main.
  • What does an outdoor spigot gauge read? A screw-on gauge shows static pressure. Very high or very low readings help us decide where to look.
  • Do you own or rent? Tenants should notify the landlord, and the landlord can authorize the visit. Same-day appointments are offered when available.

What we can and cannot settle for a San Bernardino homeowner

.

We repair and replace plumbing. We do not test water for health effects, speak for a utility or do electrical work, and we do not set permit rules. Ask the City of San Bernardino what your job needs before work begins.

Plumbing is the C-36 classification with the California Contractors State License Board. Look up any contractor's license on the CSLB website before you hire.
