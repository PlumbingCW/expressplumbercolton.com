Plumber in San Salvador Historic District, Colton, CA

.

San Salvador is one of the historic districts the City of Colton shows on its planning map. It is also one of the few where a recent Planning Commission item shows how review works in practice.

If you own a home here and a plumbing problem is on your mind, this page covers what the city has said publicly and the general steps a plumber would take. Call (866) 310-6010 to talk through a specific issue.

What a recent city agenda shows

.

A City of Colton Planning Commission agenda item, numbered HPO24-0001, covered a vacant lot on South 7th Street inside the San Salvador Historic District. The lot is in the city's Mixed Use Neighborhood zone.

The proposal was a new single-family home of about 1,316 square feet with an attached garage. The city required a Major Certificate of Appropriateness for it. That is a historic review step, and it applied to new building, not to routine repairs.

The agenda does not say what review applies to ordinary plumbing work inside an existing house. Ask the Planning Division before you assume anything.

Where the district sits

.

Real estate listings tied to the district name show a mix of streets, including South 7th Street, South La Cadena Drive, East and West lettered streets, and several streets near downtown. Listing sites also show condos, townhomes and lots, not only single-family houses.

That tells us the area is mixed. It does not give us a boundary. The city's Property Viewer is the way to check an address.

Practical plumbing steps for a district home

.

Start by working out where the problem is. A fixture that drips is a small job. A sewer backup or a wet yard is a bigger one and may involve digging.

Colton city water is hard. The city's report shows total hardness averaging about 207 mg/L, or roughly 12 grains per gallon. Faucet aerators and cartridges can clog with mineral scale, so a slow faucet is often a cleaning job before it is a replacement.

If a repair will change something visible from the street, ask first. Planning staff are at 659 North La Cadena Drive and can be reached at (909) 370-5079.

How to approach a repair in a historic district

.

A simple order of operations keeps the job on track.

  1. Describe the problem. Write down what you see, hear and smell, and where.
  2. Check the address. Use the city Property Viewer to confirm the district.
  3. Ask about review. Call Planning if the work touches the exterior.
  4. Get the permit question answered. Water heaters need one. Ask the Building Division about other work.
  5. Schedule the work. Call (866) 310-6010 to discuss the plumbing side.
