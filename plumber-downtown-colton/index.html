Plumber in Downtown Colton, CA

.

Downtown Colton is a mixed-use area, not a residential neighborhood. The city zones it for a blend of shops, offices and housing, and it applies design review to development there.

That makes this page different from our neighborhood guides. It is for owners and managers of small commercial and mixed-use buildings. Call (888) 910-8644 to talk through a specific plumbing issue.

What makes downtown different

.

The City of Colton zones its downtown as Mixed-Use Downtown and General Commercial Downtown overlay. City code says architectural and site plan review applies to all development there, and projects must fit the Colton Downtown Design Manual.

A significant part of the historic downtown was demolished in 1953 to build the Ramona Freeway, now the San Bernardino Freeway. What remains is a smaller core, so buildings vary widely in age.

Housing downtown is growing on paper

.

A City environmental document for housing and rezoning describes a proposal to allow up to 40 dwelling units per acre in the Mixed-Use Downtown zone. It is a proposal, not a finished project, and we do not know what will be built.

If more apartments arrive above shops, shared plumbing becomes more common. Shared risers and drains behave differently from a single house's pipes.

Commercial plumbing basics

.

Commercial jobs often need more planning than a house call. A plumber needs to know the building use, the number of fixtures and who owns which part of the system. Shared systems can have more than one responsible party.

If work touches the water service or meter, the City of Colton Water Utilities Department is the contact. It serves about 90 percent of the city proper. Permits go through the Building Division at 659 North La Cadena Drive, (909) 370-5079.

Before you call for a downtown building

.

Having these details speeds up the conversation.

  • Building use. Shop, restaurant, office, or mixed with apartments.
  • Which tenant is affected. Say which unit or suite has the problem.
  • Ownership. Know who is responsible, the owner or the tenant.
  • Access. Give hours when the space is open.
  • Plans. Check whether the work needs permits or design review.
