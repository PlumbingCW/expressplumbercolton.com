Why a commercial call is not a bigger house call

.

A house has one main valve and one drain line. A commercial building can have several shutoffs, shared risers, floor drains that tie into a grease interceptor, and tenants who each control a different part of the system. Finding which valve isolates which suite is often the first hour of a job.

The stakes are different too. A restroom leak in a retail space can close a store. A backed-up drain in a kitchen can stop food service. We schedule after-hours emergency dispatch for these cases, and same-day appointments are offered when available for less urgent work.

Access and approval matter as much as the repair. Many tenants do not own the pipes behind the wall, so the work may need the landlord's consent before we cut or replace anything.

The commercial calls we see most often

.

These are the situations that most often lead to a commercial service visit.

Leaks you cannot find

Water stains, a high meter reading or a wet slab with no visible source. See the leak investigation steps below for what a search can and cannot find.

Floor drains that overflow

Sewage or gray water rising at a floor drain usually means a blockage in the line, a failed trap seal or a full interceptor. Stop using nearby fixtures.

Grease trap or interceptor trouble

Slow kitchen drains, odors and backups point to a full or blocked grease trap. Regular cleaning prevents most emergencies.

Storm drain and roof drain clogs

Parking lot catch basins and roof drains fill with leaves, trash and silt. Ponding water after rain is the sign.

Lift station and pump failures

A sump or sewage lift pump that stops running can flood a basement or lower-level restroom within hours.

Backflow test due

Many businesses have a backflow assembly that must be tested on a schedule. This is planned work, not an emergency.

Backflow assembly testing: plan it before the notice goes overdue

.

A backflow prevention assembly keeps water in your building from flowing backward into the public supply. Restaurants, irrigation systems, fire sprinkler lines and some medical equipment commonly need one. The assembly has check valves and relief parts that wear, so it gets tested on a schedule.

California's cross-connection rules are part of Title 17 of the state code, and water suppliers run the program. An example ordinance from the State Water Board for small systems calls for tests at least yearly and after any installation, relocation or repair, by qualified testers. Your own water supplier sets the actual schedule and the list of testers it accepts, so ask the City of Colton water utility or whoever bills your water.

Book the test as soon as the notice arrives. A failed test means the assembly needs repair and a retest, and a supplier can act on overdue tests under its own rules. A plumber who works on commercial assemblies can repair the parts at the same visit.

Commercial leak investigation: how it goes and where it stops

.

A building leak search is a process of elimination, and it is honest to say it does not always end in one visit.

  1. Gather the clues. We ask about meter readings, wet spots, odors and when the problem began. A tenant's account is often the best starting point.
  2. Isolate by section. Valves are closed in turn to see which zone shows the loss. This narrows a large building down to one run of pipe.
  3. Test and listen. Pressure tests, acoustic listening and moisture checks locate the likely spot. Accuracy depends on access and pipe material.
  4. Open and repair. Once located, a small opening is made, the section is repaired and the area is left safe. Finishing work may be a separate trade.
  5. Confirm plumbing is the cause. Roof leaks, air conditioner condensate and exterior drainage can look like plumbing. We tell you if the source is outside our scope.

What goes into a commercial plumbing price

.

Commercial work has more variables than a home repair, so the price is set after the scope is understood. These are the main drivers.

Building size and layout. More fixtures, longer pipe runs and multiple floors take more time to trace and test.
Access and hours. Work in a food service kitchen or an occupied office may have to wait for closing time or run overnight.
Drain line length and condition. A long run with roots, scale or grease takes more passes than a short clear one.
Equipment involved. Backflow assemblies, pumps and interceptors have their own parts and testing steps.
Approvals. Landlord consent, permits and inspections can add time before work starts.
Business property policies often cover sudden and accidental water damage and often exclude wear, corrosion or slow leaks. Check your policy and your lease, take photos, and tell your landlord or manager before cleanup.

Grease trap cleaning and storm drain service in detail

.

Grease traps catch fats, oils and food solids before they reach the sewer. When the trap is full, the next dish rinse has nowhere to go, and the kitchen drains slow or back up. Cleaning means removing the lid, clearing the trapped material, checking the baffles and inspecting the pipes in and out. Your local sewer agency and the county environmental health office set the cleaning schedule and record keeping, so ask them what applies to your kitchen.

Storm drains are separate from the sewer. They carry rain off roofs and lots, and they clog with leaves, sediment and trash, especially before the first rain after a dry spell. Clearing them is a flush and pickup job, and a clear drain before a storm is cheaper than a flooded lot after one.

Floor drain overflow and lift pump repairs follow the same logic. We find out why the line stopped, restore flow, and tell you whether the underlying cause, such as a damaged line, needs sewer line repair. For backed-up lines with heavy buildup, hydro jetting scours the pipe wall better than a cable alone.

Commercial leak or sewage backup after hours: first steps

.

If water is flooding a space or sewage is rising from a floor drain, call the 24/7 line now. After-hours and weekend commercial calls are dispatched as urgent, and same-day appointments are offered when available.

  • Find and close the shutoff. Close the suite valve or the building main. If you do not know which is which, close the main and call the property manager.
  • Stop using the fixtures. Tell staff not to flush, run sinks or use the dishwasher, because that adds water to the backup.
  • Protect people and power. Keep customers and staff off wet floors and away from outlets, cords and panels.
  • Save what you can. Move inventory, files and electronics off the floor, and place barriers at doors.
  • Notify the right people. Tell the landlord or manager, and your insurer. In a food business, a sewage backup in the kitchen usually means pausing service, so contact your local environmental health office.
